Week 3. Research Literature
Studio Activities
There are 3 activities in this studio sheet (one on each page):
Activity 1: Developing a Synthesis Matrix
Activity 2: Developing a Literature Search Plan
Activity 3: Indicators of Quality
This studio focuses on the process of developing a research literature review. You will do this by working in
small groups on an ‘Activity’ related to the research area of “AI-based Chest X-ray Diagnosis” (something you
should all be able to understand quickly in broad terms).
We’re going to do this through literature review activities based on three papers on this topic. This is a typical
starting point for a literature review, where someone (e.g. a colleague or supervisor) has recommended
examples of research for you to use as a starting point for developing a research study in a particular area. In
these ‘Activities’ you will: (i) practice "reading with purpose", by reviewing these papers under time pressure;
(ii) learn to identify indicators of the quality of the work; (iii) construct a synthesis matrix for the work; and (iv)
use the papers to identify other potentially relevant articles.
The week before the studio you should study the videos, review the slides, transcript and any readings
provided and complete the quiz, which is worth just under 1% of your total marks for the unit.
During the studio you will work in small groups to complete the activities. You should aim to spend the allotted
time in the studio working on the activity (using your whiteboard) in your groups, and then reviewing with a
larger group.

Activity 1
Developing a Synthesis Matrix
This week’s studio will use the research topic of “AI-based Chest X-ray Diagnosis” to explore how to conduct
a literature search. The studio activities relate to the following papers which you should download now (see
Week 3 Moodle):
PAPER 1: Cid, Y. D., Macpherson, M., Gervais-Andre, L., Zhu, Y., Franco, G., Santeramo, R., Lim, C.,
Selby, I., Muthuswamy, K., Amlani, A., Hopewell, H., Indrajeet, D., Liakata, M., Hutchinson, C. E., Goh,
V., & Montana, G. (2024). Development and validation of open-source deep neural networks for
comprehensive chest x-ray reading: a retrospective, multicentre study. The Lancet. Digital health, 6(1),
e44–e57. https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(23)00218-2
PAPER 2: Seyyed-Kalantari, L., Zhang, H., McDermott, M.B.A. et al. Underdiagnosis bias of artificial
intelligence algorithms applied to chest radiographs in under-served patient populations. Nat Med 27,
2176–2182 (2021). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-021-01595-0
PAPER 3: M. Li, B. Lin, Z. Chen, H. Lin, X. Liang and X. Chang, "Dynamic Graph Enhanced
Contrastive Learning for Chest X-Ray Report Generation," 2023 IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer
Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), Vancouver, BC, Canada, 2023, pp. 3334-3343, doi:
10.1109/CVPR52729.2023.00325.
A synthesis matrix is a tool that we can use to help us synthesize insights from different sources and to
support our critical engagement with papers that we read. The matrix helps us not only avoid simply
describing sources in a standalone manner, but it also helps us to:
1. Compare and contrast sources.
2. Critically analyze and evaluate sources
3. Extract insights and support new perspectives.
To do this, we use a table in which we record our thoughts on the relationships between important ideas from
the various sources. A synthesis matrix is a really useful device for making sense out of multiple contributions
to ideas, differences in definitions and contrasting methods. It is a table which we can lay out in different ways
according to what we’re looking to compare and contrast. When using this approach, be careful not to just
replicate the section headings of papers. Instead, dig down into the concepts that you're exploring and use
these as the headings.
In your groups
1. Scan (i.e. skim read) the sections for each paper to establish the topic, approach and findings of each
paper (in addition to the conclusion and introduction which you should have already read); try to ignore
irrelevant aspects of the presentation that do not relate directly to the general topic “AI-based Chest X-
ray Diagnosis”.
2. Based on your readings of the papers, as a group, decide on a set of headings for columns of a
synthesis matrix (make sure you do not use headings that simply repeat the section headings of the
papers!). Keep in mind that the overall topic of research for this tutorial is “AI-based Chest X-ray
Diagnosis”.

Activity 2
Developing a Literature Search Plan
Having read a small number of papers that you are likely to have been directed to by your supervisor or a
colleague, your next step is to conduct your search for relevant literature more systematically. While industry
and student research projects in IT are unlikely to involve the conduct a formal systematic review, you can still
be systematic in your approach to the challenge of finding relevant literature for you own project.
In your groups
As a group choose either PAPER 1, PAPER 2 or PAPER 3 according to which one you believe to be most
relevant to the research area of “AI-based Chest X-ray Diagnosis”.
1. This is a 10-minute task (take no longer): Divide the literature review section of your paper between
your team members and find and read the abstracts of research papers cited in the relevant sections of
this paper (e.g. using Google Scholar). Based on what you read in these abstracts, compile a list of up
to 6 of these papers (2 per member of your group) that you consider to be candidates for a closer
reading (ones that look relevant to the topic of “AI-based Chest X-ray Diagnosis”). Use your worksheet
to record your list with a brief explanation of why each has been selected.
2. This is a 10-minute task (take no longer): Find the set of papers that have cited your paper (e.g. using
Google Scholar to find other papers that cite to paper you have chosen) and divide these between your
group members, and between you read the abstracts of these sources. Based on what you read in
these abstracts, construct a list of up to 6 papers (e.g. 2 per member of your group) that are good
candidates for a closer reading (ones that look relevant to the topic of “AI-based Chest X-ray
Diagnosis"). Use your worksheet to record your list with a brief explanation of why each has been
selected.
On return to the main session
Review you answers with your tutor and the larger group.

Activity 3
Indicators of Quality for Literature
In your groups
For each paper re-review the abstract, introduction and conclusion sections to bolster your understanding
of what the reported research is about (divide the papers between the members of your group if).
1. Work with the members of your small group to do web searches to help you understand the context in
which the research has been undertaken and published, and the response to it from the academic
community. Consider the following factors:
Venue: take time to research the venue at which the papers have been published, discuss and explore
what features of these venues will give you indicators of the quality of work published.
Citations: use Google Scholar (or other tools such as SCOPUS) to understand how much the paper has
been cited, who is citing the work, and in what publications these citations occur.
Authors: spend time exploring who the key authors are for these papers, and their track records for work
in the research area of the paper, and related areas.
2. Record your answers and be ready to informally present you findings to the larger studio group.
On return to the main session
Review with your tutor and the larger group
