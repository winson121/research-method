Faculty of Information Technology
IT Research Methods
Week 3 Part B Research Interviews
Workshop Activities
There are 4 activities in this workshop:
Activity 1: Interview topics
Activity 2: Interview schedule
Activity 3: Conducting an interview
Activity 4: Interview reﬂections
Groups created for this week's workshop should have and minimum of 3 and a maximum of 4
members
This workshop focuses on the process of the design, development, and practice of a research interview. You
will do this by working in groups to develop an interview schedule, practice the interview, and critically reﬂect
on both the interview schedule and the conduct of the interview.
Before the workshop, each week you should study the videos, review the slides, transcript and any
readings provided. Complete the weekly quiz which is worth 10% of your total marks.
During the workshop you will work in small groups to complete the activities. You should aim to spend the
allotted time in the workshop working on the activity (and writing notes) in your groups, and then reviewing
with a larger group.

Activity 1
Interview Topics
In this activity the task is to develop mini-topics, as a group, for your interview schedule.
The goal, or focus, of the interview you have to plan is:
"Grocery Shopping and Technology" - How has technology transformed the way people do grocery
shopping?
In your groups
Consider the focus of the interview and identify 5 mini topics that will be what you ask questions about
in your research interview.
The focus must be narrowed down -- as it's a big topic -- through discussion and agreement within your
group. For example, your could narrow the focus down to a number of questions about
families/travel/retail/work for the mini topics. Also, consider whether you want to talk about a single
speciﬁc country, or all countries.
Write down your mini-topics. Give each mini-topic a name, and then write a brief description of the mini-
topic and why you plan to ask questions about it.
On return to the main session
Review with the larger group

Activity 2
Interview Schedule
Once you have a high-level idea of what you want to ﬁnd out from your interviews (i.e. your mini-topics), you
need to create a set of questions, and decide on the order that you ask questions in the research interview.
This helps you to conduct your interviews consistently across multiple participants (whether you are
conducting a semi-structured or structured interview). This order of topics and questions is called an
interview schedule. Remember, it’s also important to include question(s) that invite participants to talk about
aspects of a mini-topic that you haven't anticipated, e.g. by minimizing (or excluding) the use of closed
questions.
In your groups
Using the mini-topics developed in Activity 1, develop a schedule for a 10-minute research interview to be
conducted later in the workshop.
Perform the following steps:
1. Select 2 of the mini-topics you identiﬁed in Activity 1, and develop 5-6 interview questions for each mini
topic. Remember to use a variety of question types (Video: Planning Interviews).
2. Include different types of interview question (i.e. develop a variety of questions types).
It might help to review "Strategies for Qualitative Interviews" on the Week 3 Moodle page
(particularly Page 3).
Include potential probes for each question, to elicit a more detailed response from the participant
where needed.
3. Record your interview schedule (you are going to need it) and make notes on your justiﬁcations for the
questions, probes and the order of questions.

Activity 3
Conducting and Interview
Getting good responses (i.e. valuable data) is not just about having a good intereview schedule, but depends
on you conducting the interview well - this takes practice! In this activity you will be practicing your interview
technique with your fellow students and providing feedback to each other.
In your groups
1. During this activity each group should conduct two interviews (within the group).
2. In your group, select: (i) one member to be the interviewer (the person asking the questions); (ii) one
member to be the interviewee (the person to be interviewed). The remaining members will be
observers (people making notes on how the interviewer conducts the interview).
The observer is a role we have created for this teaching and learning activity (real-world research
interviews just have an interviewer and an interviewee). The observer plays an important role by making
notes as the interview progresses which they should share with the rest of the team afterwards. Make the
observer(s) write notes so they can present this feedback to your original group.
3. Use the interviewer’s schedule that you created in Activity 2 for the interview.
Although we are not using an information sheet for this interview, and we are not asking interviewees to
sign consent forms (as it’s a teaching & learning exercise), it is still important to be clear with each other
what the data will be used for, and what will happen to it subsequently. This is the interviewer’s role before
the interview starts.
4. Conduct the interview in small group according the to roles (interviewer, interviewee, observer) that you
have agreed.
5. Once the interview is over ; change roles and repeat the interview with a different interviewer,
interviewee, and observer(s).

Activity 4
Interview Reﬂections
In your groups
1. Now that you have completed your interviews have a discussion in your small groups about:
How the interview went (in general)?
How effective of the interviewer (e.g. did they use probes, did they follow-up in answers, did they listen,
etc.)?
Could the interview schedule be improved (questions, order of questions, etc)?
2. The interviewer, interviewee, observer(s) should take turns to share their views about what worked
well, and what could be improved if the interview were to be conducted again.
3. Make notes on your observations and reﬂections and be ready to present back to the whole workshop
class.
On return to the main session
Review with the larger group
