Studio Guide
Faculty of Information Technology
IT Research and Innovation Methods
Week 3. Surveys & Questionnaires
Studio Activities
There are 3 activities in this studio sheet:
Activity 1: Question critique
Activity 2: Developing a questionnaire
Activity 3: Questionnaire feedback and note taking
This studio focuses on the process of the design, and development of an effective questionnaire. You will do
this by working in small breakout groups on a set of ‘activities’.
The first activity requires you to work in small groups to critique the wording of some sample
questionnaire questions, to help you understand some pitfalls of selecting the right wording and forms
of questions.
The second activity require you to work in small groups to develop suitable topics of exploration for
questionnaires and designing suitable questions for questionnaires: adjusting to student life with a
particular focus on your engagement with the local community.
In the third activity you will listen to group presentations, take down notes individually regarding the
ways other groups can improve their questionnaires.
The week before the studio you should study the videos, review the slides, transcript and any readings
provided and complete the quiz, which is worth just under 1% of your total marks for the unit.
During the studio you will work in small groups to complete the activities. You should aim to spend the allotted
time in the studio working on the activity (using your whiteboard) in your groups, and then reviewing with a
larger group.

Activity 1
Question Critique
In your groups
Part A: Critiquing Questions
Read the 10 questions below and discuss any problems with the wording of these questions. Make notes
on your conclusions and suggestions for improvements.
1. What is wrong with young people these days and what can we do about it?
2. Do you go swimming? [1] Never, [2] Rarely; [3] Frequently; [4] Sometimes
3. What do you think can be done about global warming?
4. Most medical professionals think that smoking causes lung cancer; do you: [a]Strongly Agree; [b]
Agree; [c] Neither agree nor disagree; [d] Disagree; [e] Strongly disagree.
5. Do you agree that students should not have to take an exam at the end of their course? [a] Yes; [b] No.
6. Has your son ever stolen anything? If so when?
7. What do you think about the left-wing media’s attempt to blackmail the Government?
8. How much food do you think the average family throws away in a week?
9. How much do you earn?
10. What is your ethnicity?
Part B: Improving questions
Discuss how you would improve the questions, making notes about what these would be. Appoint a group
member to communicate your findings when asked.
Part C: Class Feedback
Be ready to present back your findings to another groups and the rest of the studio class.
On return to the main session
Review with the larger group.
Individually

Activity 2
Developing the questionnaire
Activity 2 consists of two parts:
Part A: developing topics
Part B: developing questions
In your smaller groups you will be developing 2 topics from an area of focus provided in the studio and then
you will develop 4 survey questions, for each individual topic.
In your groups
Part A: Developing Topics
The area of concern for the survey that you are going to develop is:
Going to university is a major life change for many people. We are interested in how people adjust
to this new social situation (student life).
You should interpret this area concern according to your own personal experiences.
1. The first task in the design of the questionnaire is to develop the range topics to explore for the area of
concern; these are the factors that you believe are likely to be important. Topics should be broad
enough that you can easily imagine asking 4 or more questions about each one.
e.g. “Balancing your studies with your social life” is one such topic.
2. Spend 10 minutes discussing what these topics (related to the area of concern) should be; start with the
collective experiences of members of your group, and combine this with your “rapid” literature review
skills to quickly identify what research has been done on this in this area previously.
3. In addition to questions about demographic information (nationality, age, course, etc.) identify 2 topics
that you believe to be either important, or interesting, to explore about people's experiences of adjusting
to student life.
Make notes on your discussion and answers to these tasks.
On return to the main session
Short review of progress so far before returning to groups.

Activity 3
Questionnaire Critique and Feedback
In this activity, a member of your group (selected in Activity 2) will present the group’s questions and
justifications from Activity 2 back to another group. Each presentation should take no more than 2-3 minutes
per group, followed by 10-15 minutes of critique and feedback.
Individually
During the presentations by each group, make notes on as many of the proposed questions as possible (a
minimum of 2), and provide feedback and critique for the presenter (write down some points and feedback
suggestions).
In your groups
After the presentations, you will return to your small groups and you should spend 10 minutes discussing
and reviewing the feedback, and making improvements to your initial questions.
On return to the main session
You will be invited to share you thoughts, feedback you have received, and any improvement with the rest
of the class.
