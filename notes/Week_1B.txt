Weekly Communication Activity
This workshop focuses on understanding what research ethics means, and the responsibilities you have in
research to protect the interests of your participants and their data when you are conducting research. As you
will have discovered in the online video for weeks 1 part a and b, “research” can mean many different things
in IT, and researchers approach problems using different research designs depending on their subﬁeld and
the 
research questions that they are looking to answer. Research ethics can also pose different challenges
depending on the design of the study and types of people and data involved.

Assessments page.

readings provided. Complete the weekly quiz which is worth 5% of your total marks.

allotted time in the workshop working on the activity (and writing notes) in your groups, and then reviewing

Activity 1
Dimensions of Research Ethics
Work in groups, then review as a larger group with you tutor
Activity 1 involves thinking about the dimensions of ethics research introduced in the week’s videos.
“Dimensions of ethical research” (see the Week 1 video, slides, and transcript) outlines how the principles and 
values translate into a set of dimensions of ethical research. In this activity you should consider what these 
dimensions could mean in practice, how consideration of them might shape decisions you make about a 
research design, and how an ethics review committee might consider them when assessing a design of 
yours.
In your groups
1. For each of the dimensions that follow, come up with a (hypothetical but plausible) example of an
aspect of a research design, or an activity within a research design, for an IT research project that might
be cause for concern (i.e. not speciﬁcally an actual project but one that you imagine an IT researcher
might want to conduct):
i. Physical or psychological harm or abuse
ii. Informed consent
iii. Right of withdrawal
iv. Vulnerable participant groups
v. Deception
vi. Coercion
vii. Illegal practices
viii. Invasion of privacy
ix. Conﬁdentiality
2. Write down your examples on your worksheet, with brief explanations.
⚐ Make notes on your shared worksheet.
On return to the main session
Review with your tutor and the larger group.
Take a 10-minute break! (before the next activity)

Activity 2
Reviewing Actual Research for Ethical Issues
Work in groups, then review as a larger group with your tutor
Consider the paper introduced in Week 1 as an example of a “simulation study”:
Hudson, S., Fogarty, J., Atkeson, C., Avrahami, D., Forlizzi, J., Kiesler, S., Lee, J. and Yang, J., 2003,
April. Predicting human interruptibility with sensors: a Wizard of Oz feasibility study. In Proceedings of
the SIGCHI Conference on Human factors in Computing Systems (pp. 257-264).
In this project Hudson and his colleagues explored the question of how interruptible people (ofﬁce workers)
are, and whether the activities people were in involved in were a reliable predictor of the best time to interrupt
them. However, the activities they wanted to take account of were very wide ranging, from speaking, writing,
sitting, standing, and being on-the-phone, to things like whether they were using a keyboard, using a mouse,
looking at a monitor and a range of other things.
Key elements of the research design included: (a) that rather than develop sensors for detecting the different
activities they simply used a camera and microphone to capture people working in an ofﬁce; (b) after the
capture the researchers performed a manual analysis of the data to established when the activities were
occurring (i.e. simulated the sensors); and (c) that subjects were given an audio prompt, in response to which
they had to self-report their interpretability.
In your groups
1
. Spend 5-10 minutes working in groups to (re)familiarise yourself with the research; don’t read the whole
paper, just the “Introduction” and “Study Details” sections.
2
. Consider the dimensions of research ethics (only the relevant ones) in relation to the study design as
follows (record your considerations on the worksheet):
i
. Identify different aspects of the research design (as reported in the paper) that have the potential to
cause a concern in relation to the different ethics dimensions.
ii
. For each aspect, describe how (if at all) the authors explain (in the paper) how they accounted for
the potential concern.
iii
. For each aspect, explain how you would have ensured that the potential concern is addressed
(where you consider this is possible).
⚐ Make notes on your shared worksheet.
On return to the main session
Review your answers with your tutor and the larger group.

Activity 3
Processes, People & Documentation
Work in groups, then review as a larger group with you tutor
Careful consideration of ethical issues related to the design of a research project must be backed up by
clearly documented processes. A human research ethics committee will want to see this documentation.
Consider the scenario in which you have been asked to repeat Hudson et al’s (2003) study on interruptibility
as a research project at Monash University using 20 ofﬁce workers that are members of staff of the University.
To do this you will ﬁrst need to develop and submit a research proposal to the Monash University Human
Research Ethics Committee, and in preparation for this submission you must think through all the ethical
issues, how you will address them, and what documentation you will need to ensure that the study is
conducted as you propose.
In your groups
Consider the following processes that will be part of your study design: (1) recruitment; (2) conduct of
observations and data capture; (3) data analysis; and (4) data curation.
Working in groups, use the worksheet provided to outline what each will involve, to answer the following
questions:
1. who the people that are involved in each process (1-4) will be?
2. what the processes (1-4) will involve, and for each are there are ethical considerations that need to be
addressed?
3. what documents will be helpful (or that Monash requires you to create) to create to capture these
processes, and who will need to be aware of these documents?
⚐Make notes on your shared worksheet.
On return to the main session
Review you answers with your tutor and the larger group.
⚐This is a large task (to do properly) and would typically take an experienced researcher of a few weeks to
complete (including creating the documents), so you should focus on covering what you see as the main
and most interesting points relating to each process (recruitment, conduct of observations and data
capture; data analysis, and data curation).
