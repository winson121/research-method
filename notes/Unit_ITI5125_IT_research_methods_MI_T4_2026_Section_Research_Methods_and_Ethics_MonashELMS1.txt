Research Methods and Ethics

This week, you will be introduced to IT research methods and its ethical aspects. In particular, you will study the importance of IT research and its various research designs either in industry or academia. Moreover, you will study the principles and values of ethical research and understand the process of institutional ethics review.

Week's Learning Outcomes:

Identify the purpose and importance of IT research.

Place IT research in a context of pure and applied research.

Understand various research designs as they have been applied in IT research.

Characterise the qualities of an IT research question.

Understand and apply the principles and values of ethical research.

Understand and apply the dimensions of human subject research ethics.

Understand the process and procedures of institutional ethics review.

Understand the notion of data custodianship and its application in human subject research.

Research methods are not just for academic research - read on below to find out more. In this unit we will introduce a variety of such research methods and each week will highlight them at the top of the Moodle page, like below:

Creating a Research Question

Methods for Creating Knowledge

The unit is called ���Research Methods for IT��� - which admittedly makes you think right away of stuffy academic research conducted in universities, where academics spend all day in thoughtful repose alone in their offices, coming up with strange new theories related to IT.
Rightly so, most of you will be immediately thinking: ���that���s not where my career is going, how is this relevant to me!���. The word research here is confusing ��� it just means asking questions���asking questions about anything, and then planning an intervention and analysis to try and answer that question.
In fact, the idea that there are unknowns in the IT sector and that we need to conduct activities to find out more about something (creating knowledge about it) is core to how innovation happens across the entire IT sector. So, in fact ��� when we talk about research methods ��� we are actually talking about the skills and practices that create knowledge that inform the day-to-day innovation that happens in companies across the world.
Questions such as:
���How do you know if changing the UI on your e-commerce site will affect user dwell-time?���, 
���How is your new search algorithm performing compared to competitors or your old version?���, 
���How reliable is my re-developed image recognition tool for my customers���, or
���What are the core challenges my users are currently facing���
are all ���research questions��� and therefore require the selection and use of appropriate methods to answer them.
Everyone from huge international players in the IT sector such as SAP, Google and Amazon to tiny start-ups make use of numerous methods to answer questions about their customers, their products and their business practices. Most importantly, the use of these methods is not limited to the few companies that are able to sustain a specific ���research group���, but in fact are used throughout the day-to-day software development lifecycle.
In this unit, we will rapidly expose you to a cross-section of the core research methods that are commonly applied within the IT sector, giving you a flavour of the types of methods available, and some of the wider concerns to be aware of when you are involved in creating knowledge within an organisation.
Learn more about the relationship between research and innovation here

https://www.eitdigital.eu/newsroom/news/archive/article/the-saga-of-research-vs-innovation/
 

Learning methods and practising skills

Our aim in this unit is to equip you with an awareness of, and some experience with a number of key research methods that are widely useful in the IT sector. In each week of the unit we will be covering a number of different methods, split into 4 categories --  Framing your research (including framing, ethics and literature); Qualitative methods ��� which are used when asking questions of people directly (such as interviews, focus groups and questionnaires);  Quantitative methods ��� used when you need to analyse and answer questions on numerical data (includes useful statistical approaches, and an understanding of machine learning) and lastly how to communicate and protect the new knowledge that is generated. 
The pre-material which we have recorded as short videos are there to give you a basic understanding of the methods involved, and where and how they can be applied. You will then put some of these methods and skills into practice during a facilitated workshop each week where tutors will guide you in structure activities. Lastly, we will be holding a weekly ���town hall��� session where we will be taking questions from the online forums and in-person classes allowing you a chance to get answers to the burning questions that have emerged over the week.
In each week���s content, you will see that we have highlighted a few key methods that exemplify what each area is used for, with some examples.

Introduction

Ethics are an integral part of using research methods both in industry and academia. In very few scenarios will you be asking a research question that does not incorporate some form of real data from real people. This could be log data captured anonymously from an online system about UX interactions, user preferences from existing profiles on a website, medical records from a health system or recordings of interviews from focus groups. 
As soon as you start doing this ��� and real people are involved ��� you need to consider the ethical implications of what you are doing. Being thorough, however, does not necessarily mean more paperwork and more work. Often, it���s simply a matter of asking the right questions of your methods, mode of data collection and how you will process the data.

Ethical Assessment/Planning
: This is a core supporting method, which although does not allow you to answer a research question directly, it helps you correctly frame your question, ascertain if you are obtaining the right information (or any additional information), and most importantly makes you think about the fundamental reasons for conducting the research.

Case Study

John is an engineer who works within the Software Engineering division of Microsoft in a team designing and manufacturing AI-based home assistants. John���s team was tasked with improving their flagship product, ���Cortana���, by gearing it towards a new user demographic: seniors living in nursing homes. After conducting an extensive survey of the existing smart assistants for nursing homes and their features, the team noticed that there are no current products that offer the feature of reminding users to take their medication. John and his team are designing a plan to conduct market research to find out to what extent such a feature would be useful for this user group. They decide that the best way to go about conducting this research would be through observing a group of their existing users within this age group, and conducting interviews and focus group discussions with them. Both the observations and interviews would be conducted in person and video recorded to be analysed at a later stage. Additionally, the team would also need to gain access to their clients��� medical records to understand what reminders would be required.
John understands that observing and collecting data from this age group may raise complex questions as their users could be described as a vulnerable group. In addition, obtaining access to their participants��� medical records can be tricky as medical records contain sensitive personal information. So he and his team put extra effort into creating proper documentation such as explanatory statements to communicate the research to participants and consent forms in a format and language that are accessible and easily understandable to their older participants. John���s team made sure the language used in the documentation was straightforward and contained no technical jargon. They decided to recruit research assistants with extensive training and experience conducting research with older people. The research assistants are tasked with reading the documentation out loud to participants prior to starting data collection to make sure that the participants fully understand and consent to take part. They will also answer any questions the participants may have and make sure that they are aware of their right to withdraw should they no longer wish to participate. To ensure his team���s market research is conducted ethically, John has decided to consult the company���s ���Aether��� committee* to ensure that everything is covered in the research design and any potential ethical issues are appropriately addressed. The Aether committee board listens to his team���s proposal and requests to see the documentation they had prepared. The committee board advised the team to ensure that their participants are aware of where their data is going to be kept and what happens to it after the research ends.
*Aether, which stands for AI, Ethics and Effects in Engineering and Research, is an advisory board at Microsoft that deliberates about questions, issues, and challenges arising with developing and fielding applications of AI.

About the Workshop

Workshops are all 3 hours in length and are delivered either online using Zoom or in-person on campus. Your lecturers/tutors will have contacted you to introduce themselves and to share the Zoom link (if applicable) for the weekly workshop.

Contact the lecturer if you have any questions about the timing of a workshop or require a Zoom link for your workshop. This information is also available on Moodle.

Pre-workshop material

Each workshop is a collaborative space designed to give you a space to explore and practice new research skills. You will participate in activities that are based directly on the pre-material on this page. Before this weeks workshop you should:

Consume

You need to watch the videos (before the workshop): These aim to present content in small (5-10 minute) chunks and are subtitled.

Reflect

You should review the content: Slides and transcripts are provided for all the videos, reading these can help you understand the content in your own time.

Engage

You should engage with the associated reading: There are various resources and papers, that we'll be pointing you to before and during Week 2, so take some time to review these.

Participate

Post into the discussion board: if you have questions or insights that you want to share please don't hesitate to post these, (also if you have any questions about the unit).
