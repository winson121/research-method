What a literature review doesIt provides the background needed to understand the study:•terminology and techniques.Explains the theoretical and practical basis for the study:•questions that underlie the project,•outlines important issues, needs and gaps, •sets out the theoretical and practical framework.Related research is:•synthesised from relevant published work•summarized to identify recent advances and their implications (ie. the “state of the art”)•explored to find the boundaries of previous workSource: https://guides.lib.monash.edu/geosciences/theses

Arrangement of materialsOne area new researchers find difficult is to work out the order and sequence of materials.There are a number of ways to do this, two common types are:•Chronological•ThematicEach has its strengths and weaknesses.Students often start chronologically because it matches the process of reading in the field, however often transition to thematic, as their conceptual model of the problem develops.Common practice (in presentation) is a mixture of the two, that is, to organize things thematically with chronological structure with themes.
===== PAGE 6 =====
Chronological literature review“In a literature review organized chronologically, you group and discuss your sources in order of their publication date, highlighting the changes in research in the field and your specific topic over time.”Allows you to easily understand how ideas have developed and builds up to where you define what your contribution will be.One weakness is that it can take some time to get to the latest research and the most important concepts for your research project.In Information Technology and Computer Science this is not generally considered an appropriate form.
http://www.academiccoachingandwriting.org/dissertation-doctor/dissertation-doctor-blog/iv-the-structure-of-your-literature-review/
===== PAGE 7 =====
Thematic literature review“In a review organized thematically, you group and discuss your sources in terms of the themes, theoretical concepts, and topics that either you decide are important to understanding your topic or that you have identified from reviewing the key studies on your topic.” This structure allows you more flexibility in that you decide which concepts are most relevant to your research project and the order you will discuss them.Usually indicates a greater level of synthesis of the materials and the latest research can be introduced at any time.

General guidelines (stand alone literature review)A. INTRODUCTION•Defines and identifies the topic and establishes the reason for the literature review.•Points to general trends in what has been published about the topic.•Explains the criteria used in analysing and comparing articles.B. BODY OF THE REVIEW•Groups articles into thematic clusters, or subtopics. Clusters may be grouped together chronologically, thematically, or methodologically.•Proceeds in a logical order from cluster to cluster.•Emphasizes the main findings or arguments of the articles in the student’s own words. Keeps quotations from sources to an absolute minimum.C. CONCLUSION•Summarizes the major themes that emerged in the review and identifies areas of controversy in the literature.•Pinpoints strengths and weaknesses among the articles (innovative methods used, gaps in research, problems with theoretical frameworks, etc.).•Concludes by formulating questions that need further research within the topic, and provides some insight into the relationship between that topic and the larger field of study or discipline. http://www.brooklyn.cuny.edu/web/aca_facultywac/Mini-Lessons-LiteratureReview.pdf
