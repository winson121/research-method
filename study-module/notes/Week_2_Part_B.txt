Activity 1
Forming a Research Question & Selecting Methods
Forming a research question is a core skill in conducting research. Often you are provided with only a very
high-level idea of what the research problem is, and you will need to reﬁne this into a number of distinct and
answerable questions that can lead to insight.
Make sure that you and your team fully understand the following scenario:
You work at a company which provides student administration service infrastructure to universities.
Their main product offering is a suite of tools that links together the student enrollment process, from
initial interest in the institution, through course and unit allocation, timetabling, transcript management
and ﬁnance management. As such, the product is visible and used to many different classes of user,
including prospective students, current students, teaching staff and administrative staff. As with many
complex systems, as the company has grown, they have inherited and integrated a wide variety of
systems from other vendors, to create a rich product offering. However, little is understood internally
about how effective these systems are at working together. As the team responsible for systems
integration at the company, your team has been tasked with understanding where the opportunities are
for improving the infrastructure. In the company's words, they want to see increased system efﬁciency,
and improved user experience.
In this activity, you will work through a research planning process. However, as in many
organisations, teams do not work in isolation and you will have to continue on from another team’s
work at each stage.
For this scenario, these stages are:
1. Selecting a research focus - there are many different ways to approach the problem, and no piece of
research can answer all possible questions. Focus on an element that you want to investigate and
create 2 distinct research questions.
2. Identify existing data that you can use to answer these questions.
3. Identify additional data required from people. What types of methods (e.g. interviews) will you need to
employ to collect this data?
4. Select participant group(s) - Whether collecting qualitative or quantitative data, you will have to be
selective about who you collect from and what you collect, so come up with some criteria for your
participants and data collection.
In your groups
At each stage, you should start by reviewing any notes already made by a previous team.
You will then continue where they left off with the next stage, making notes on the sheet.
After 10 mins, you will pass on your notes to an adjacent team.
⚐Make detailed notes on your shared worksheet.

Activity 2
Creating a Research Plan
At the end of the last activity, you will have ended up with the outline of a research plan. This plan has been
ratiﬁed by senior management in the company and your team has been tasked with operationalising the
research. However, as is always the case, you have been given a limited amount of time to complete the
research and produce some ﬁndings, since management wants to present your team’s ﬁndings at the annual
general meeting in 3 months.
Your task is to plan this research within these constraints.
In your groups
In this activity, produce a plan representing the various stages of the research, considering:
1. Individual activities that will need to be conducted during the stages, e.g. recruitment advertising,
recruitment shortlisting, interview scheduling making sure to identify any dependencies between them.
2. What design or piloting may be required for each stage.
3. What key resources (people, time, expertise) will be required to conduct this work?
⚐Make notes on your shared worksheet.

Activity 3
Resource Allocation
The resources available to conduct research in any context are always constrained. Part of the skill in
conducting effective research is to use available resources effectively.
Since your team is already working on an active project, management has allocated you $3000 for you to
outsource or contract your research, using the company's list of suppliers and temporary contract staff.
Your task is to resource your research plan from the last task to the best of your ability, given your
initial set of resources.
⚐Remember that effective research often triangulates ﬁndings from multiple sources, and that these
ﬁndings need to be effectively communicated to the AGM.
In your groups
Each task you might want to resource has a ﬁnite cost, listed in the table below. You may ﬁnd that you will
not be able to resource your entire plan, so you must select which parts of your plan to keep, which parts to
adjust and which to drop, whilst also considering how detailed or nuanced you need your ﬁndings to be in
order to answer your research question.
